Method, equations and uncertainty

1. The accounting identity

Employment in year t is defined as:

E(t) = max[0, E(2026) − E(2026) × q × a × s × w(t) + N × w(t)]

E(2026) = 1.96 million, an industry projection used as the model's fixed initial condition; q = 0.58, an author assumption for the share of task-equivalent BPO work commercially vulnerable, not the ILO economy-wide occupational exposure estimate; a = adoption fraction; s = seat-substitution fraction when adopted; N = net new/sustained roles in millions. w(t) is an openly declared adoption-progress schedule: 2026=0, 2027=.07, 2028=.17, 2029=.50, 2030=1, 2031=1.18, 2032=1.36. It is deliberately heuristic; values after 2030 are continuations, not new calibrations.

2. Revenue and household indices

Sector revenue uses a compound annual change: R(t) = 42.3 × (1 + g)^(t − 2026) in billions of US dollars. Revenue per employee is 1000 × R(t)/E(t) in dollars. It is not a worker salary, national welfare figure or productivity estimate of the entire economy.

The illustrative median real-wage index uses 100 × (1 + r)^(t − 2026). Local capture illustrates how a hypothetical $100M of incremental AI service margin could be split between domestic and external claimants; it is not computed from sector revenue. The remittance index follows a separate authored 2030 endpoint for each path, interpolated exponentially. We do not use these to invent an FX rate or mechanically infer GDP: doing so requires a coherent balance-of-payments, production and policy model that this site does not claim to have.

3. What remains uncertain

Enterprises automate tasks, not necessarily entire jobs. Savings depend on escalation, software integration, regulatory restrictions and customer tolerance. Labor supply responds through new roles, wage adjustments, migration, reduced hours and informal work. AI can also stimulate new service demand. Domestic value capture depends on ownership and import leakage. These are partly correlated outcomes, not independent dice rolls.

The original dossier's 13% / 66% / 21% labels resulted from assumed input distributions and threshold classifications. They cannot be interpreted as calibrated probabilities for a political-economic crisis with currency, robotics and financial shocks. This redesigned site therefore treats the three futures as **conditional narratives**, not as a weighted predictive distribution. See the independent review checklist and audit notes.

4. Known limitations

  • 2026 baseline is an IBPAP outlook, not a realized year-end count; 2025 values are rounded industry totals. The model is conditional from that start.
  • q, adoption, substitution, job creation, local capture, the annual time weights, revenue growth and household indices are illustrative and not fitted to an econometric sample.
  • The three scenarios are not a probability-weighted distribution. Slider outputs are counterfactual arithmetic, not central estimates.
  • Indirect employment losses, wage distribution, occupation transition costs, import/export price elasticities, remittance composition, interest-rate responses and reserve management are not estimated here.
  • Published IBPAP 2028 outlook forms an essential external reality check, not a mathematical constraint on future adverse scenarios.
